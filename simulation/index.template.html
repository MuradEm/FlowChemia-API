Experiment settings

A rating at least 1.5 stars from the weighted median is provisionally discounted only after six reviewer-week units exist. Three similar recent reviewer-week units restore its influence. These thresholds are experiments.

Experimental: compare each reviewer with peers rating the same skills and colleagues around the same time. Learning excludes the colleague currently being scored and all chemistry answers.

Needs at least eight matched reviewer-week units across three other colleagues. Estimates shrink toward zero. A relative tendency is not an explanation of someone's motives.

How this candidate works
  1. Ratings use 1–5. Internal points map 1 to 0 and 5 to 100. There is no zero-star answer.
  2. Unobserved answers supply no numeric evidence. No evidence means blank, not 50.
  3. Weight decays by half each selected half-life. Older evidence remains available; this experiment has no hard expiry.
  4. One reviewer contributes one averaged evidence unit per colleague, dimension and calendar week. Same-week repeats do not create independent confirmations.
  5. Unusual high and low answers follow the same rule. Support means ratings within 0.75 stars during the latest 42 days.
  6. Three matching reviewer-week units restore weight; they may come from different colleagues or the same colleague over different weeks. This is recurrence, not proof of truth.
  7. Scores recompute when time or evidence changes. No survey is deleted or labelled false.

Why reviewer identity matters here

Three weeks from Carla count as three similar reviewer-week units, just as reports from three colleagues do. They restore full influence after the third round. Later, the weighted median can move and older high ratings can themselves become discounted. Neither formula currently distinguishes independent corroboration from one person’s persistent experience.

The stable-skill assumption in Carla-only cases tests sensitivity to one persistent disagreement. The real-change assumption in several-reviewer cases tests responsiveness. These are synthetic labels; the formulas never receive them. One colleague may observe a real problem, and several may share a bias. The ratings do not establish which explanation is true.

High-rating cases mirror only Alice’s historical technical ratings (1↔5, 2↔4). Her actual saved card and completed survey are untouched. This creates an equally surprising positive case rather than treating a normal 5-star report for Alice as an anomaly.

Known-skill benchmark

Twenty repeatable seeds for each scenario. Error is the mean absolute distance from the fictional current skill level, in internal 0–100 points. Lower is better. Truth labels are available only to the evaluator, never the scoring functions. Results use default settings; the sliders above do not rerun this benchmark.

The comparison includes failure cases. Relative calibration cannot identify a harsh scale shared by everyone, and cannot reliably separate skill from friendship-driven answers. No correction is adopted for production.
